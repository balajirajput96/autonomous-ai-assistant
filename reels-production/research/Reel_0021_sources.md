# Reel 0021 — Naya context, purani aadat: kya badal sakta hai?

**Topic:** Habit discontinuity after a major context change.  
**Claim class:** Habit-theory evidence plus observational/field findings; not a universal behaviour-change guarantee.  
**Audience question:** “क्या घर, job, route या routine बदलने पर पुरानी habit अपने-आप टूट जाती है?”

## Evidence-bounded claims

| Proposed reel claim | Evidence boundary | Source support |
|---|---|---|
| Repeating a behaviour in stable circumstances can make the response more automatic and cue-linked. | This describes a habit mechanism, not every repeated behaviour or a fixed brain trait. | Thomas, Poortinga & Sautkina review the context-cued account; Stojanovic et al. test context stability during intentional habit building. [1] [2] |
| A major change of setting can be a moment when a previously cue-linked routine is more open to deliberate reconsideration. | “Can be” matters: the cited evidence concerns particular behaviours and contexts, not a promise of effortless change. | Verplanken et al. examine recent home moves and commuting; Thomas et al. report related UK survey findings. [1] [3] |
| Stable contexts supported greater automaticity and goal attainment in a six-week study-habit experiment and an app-data study. | This finding is about forming new intentional habits; it does not directly prove that every disrupted habit disappears. | Stojanovic, Grund & Fries (2022). [2] |
| After a move, a useful question is: “मेरे पुराने cue अब कौन-से हैं, और मैं नए routine में किस small action को जोड़ सकता/सकती हूँ?” | This is a low-stakes reflection prompt, not treatment or a behaviour-change prescription. | It follows the cue/context framework above. [1] [2] [3] |

## Limits that must remain in the script

> A context change does **not** “reset the brain,” automatically remove an unwanted habit, or guarantee that a new routine will last. It can also disrupt helpful routines. The available studies include travel choices, study habits and observational/field designs; causal effects and duration differ by behaviour and setting.

## Source notes

1. **Thomas, Poortinga & Sautkina (2016), _PLOS ONE_.** Analysed a UK survey of 18,053 respondents. The abstract reports that, among people with stronger environmental views, car use was lower only after a recent home move, with a pattern that diminished as time since moving increased. This is an association in a commuting context, not evidence that moving causes a general habit cure. [1]
2. **Stojanovic, Grund & Fries (2022), _Frontiers in Psychology_.** A six-week student study (95 participants; 2,482 repetitions) and app-data analysis reported that context stability predicted more automaticity and higher habit-repetition goal attainment. The reel may use this to explain why context cues matter, not to estimate a universal timetable. [2]
3. **Verplanken et al. (2008), _Journal of Environmental Psychology_.** This peer-reviewed travel-mode study describes the habit-discontinuity hypothesis and compares university employees who had recently moved with those who had not. It is useful as an early field example, but is limited to the studied population and transport domain. [3]
