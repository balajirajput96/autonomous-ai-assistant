# Reel 0022 — If–then plan: इरादा कब action बनता है?

**Topic:** Implementation intentions.  
**Claim class:** Goal-setting and behaviour-change evidence; not a guarantee that planning produces action.  
**Audience question:** “क्या ‘अगर X होगा, तो मैं Y करूँगा/करूँगी’ वाला plan सच में मदद कर सकता है?”

## Evidence-bounded claims

| Proposed reel claim | Evidence boundary | Source support |
|---|---|---|
| An implementation intention is an if–then plan that links a specific situation or opportunity with a goal-directed response. | It is more specific than merely naming a goal; it does not replace the underlying goal or resources needed to act. | National Cancer Institute construct summary. [1] |
| The strategy aims to help people notice and use a planned opportunity to act. | The NCI summary says effects are stronger when a self-regulatory problem is present and the goal intention is strong and activated. The reel must include this condition. | NCI summary. [1] |
| A frequently cited meta-analysis across 94 studies reported a medium-to-large average association/effect on goal attainment. | Do not turn the average `d = .65` into a promise for an individual, a percentage chance of success, or proof that every plan works. | NCI summary of Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006). [1] |
| Evidence varies by behaviour and intervention design. | A systematic review/meta-analysis of adult physical-activity randomized trials reported a non-significant overall pooled result, moderate heterogeneity, and a positive subgroup finding when the strategy was reinforced. | da Silva et al. (2018). [2] |
| A practical example is “If I finish lunch, then I will put my walking shoes by the door,” provided the cue is realistic and the action is small and feasible. | This is an educational illustration, not health treatment, a prescription, or a guaranteed outcome. | The if–then structure follows the construct definition. [1] |

## Limits that must remain in the script

> An if–then plan is not a productivity hack that overrides illness, access, time, safety, motivation, or structural barriers. It cannot guarantee action, and the physical-activity review found that the overall pooled effect in its included trials was not statistically significant. The spoken script may explain the idea but must not promise “you will definitely become consistent.”

## Source notes

1. **National Cancer Institute, Implementation Intentions construct summary.** Defines implementation intentions as if–then plans connecting a situational cue to an effective response. The page summarizes Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) and reports `d = .65` for overall goal attainment across 94 studies, with effects moderated by self-regulatory difficulty and strong activated goals. This is a government research summary, not a replacement for reading the original chapter. [1]
2. **da Silva et al. (2018), _PLOS ONE_.** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials of implementation intentions for adult physical activity. The extracted text reports moderate heterogeneity (`I² = 54%`), an overall pooled result of `0.15` (95% CI `-0.01` to `0.31`), and a positive reinforced-strategy subgroup. This supports a clear “results vary by context and reinforcement” limitation. [2]
3. **Wieber, Thürmer & Gollwitzer (2015), _Frontiers in Human Neuroscience_.** Open-access review explains implementation intentions as plans designed to help translate goal intentions into action and discusses mechanisms and boundary conditions. It is used only for conceptual background; the reel makes no neural-causation claim. [3]
